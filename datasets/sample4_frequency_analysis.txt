Frequency analysis is a technique used to break substitution ciphers by studying the
frequency of letters or groups of letters in a ciphertext. The technique relies on
the fact that in any given language certain letters and combinations of letters occur
with characteristic frequencies.

In English text, the most common letters in order of frequency are:
E T A O I N S H R D L C U M W F G Y P B V K J X Q Z

The letter E alone accounts for approximately 12.7 percent of all letters in typical
English text. The letters T and A follow at roughly 9.1 and 8.2 percent respectively.

Common digraphs include TH, HE, IN, ER, AN, RE, and ON. Common trigraphs include
THE, AND, ING, HER, and HAT. These patterns remain consistent across large bodies
of text and provide powerful tools for the cryptanalyst.

By comparing the frequency distribution of a ciphertext against the expected frequency
distribution of the source language, a cryptanalyst can make educated guesses about
the substitution mapping and gradually recover the plaintext.
